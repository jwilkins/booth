Progress · 0.1.1 · 29 October 2026

Ten releases
in eight weeks.
Here is where it is.

Booth has gone from writing its first drive to placing cue points from the words a record sings, and to a drive a CDJ-3000 plays, in eight weeks. This page is the honest state of it — including the parts that are written but not demonstrated, which on a page like this are the parts that matter.

01 — What works today

Demonstrated, on hardware or in the test suite

  • A CDJ-3000 on firmware 3.22, a CDJ-3000X on firmware 1.40 and a CDJ-1500X on firmware 1.10 all browse and play a drive this writes, each from its own slot — the CDJ-3000 from the legacy export.pdb, which is the only database it reads, and over Pro DJ Link as well; the other two from exportLibrary.db. Playlists, track list, key search, colour and three-band waveforms, beat grid, hot cues and phrases on a loaded track, with nothing rekordbox has touched on the stick.
  • Writes a failing stick cannot leave half done: every file landed whole and read back off the device past the cache, the audio compared byte for byte, a write that will not fit refused before it starts, and a write that fails stopped with the databases left as the last good one made them.
  • An audit of any drive, whoever wrote it: where its two databases disagree, and for each generation of player what it will not open or will not find.
  • Both on-drive databases from one collection, written in one pass so they cannot disagree, and read back and checked afterwards.
  • Analysis for every generation — .DAT, .EXT and .2EX — written from one set of measurements.
  • Tempo, beat grid, key, phrases, cues and colour waveforms, all measured locally. A record at one tempo comes out on one straight grid on its kick; one that really moves is gridded moving. Tempo is measured against madmom and Essentia: on 75 CC records Booth has the tempo they agree on, or a listener counted, on 58 of 62.
  • The genre, one of Discogs's 400 styles, heard by a model you install, and with it which octave the tempo is in: hip hop at 86, drum and bass at 172, from a table of 76 club genres and the tempos they are played at. A file's own genre tag does the same without the model.
  • A beat grid corrected by hand with the controls a player has had for twenty years — halve, double, nudge, slide a beat onto the playhead, say which beat is the one — or by marking beats and letting a grid be fitted to them.
  • Loudness normalisation to EBU R128, by re-encoding or by ReplayGain tags with the audio left untouched.
  • Stems — vocals, melody and drums — rendered ahead of time with demucs or a RoFormer, and carried to the drive as tracks sorted under their parent.
  • Hot cues that mean the same on every record: A the hook, B the main break, C the hook in a drop, D the main drop, and E to H what a set needs next.
  • Cues from the words: the record, cut to where its vocal stem sings, through a speech recogniser, the words read for repetition, the hook cued at its best airing on the bar line before it, every memory cue carrying its line, and a .lrc beside the track.
  • Lyrics looked up before they are listened for, taken outright when the evidence is strong, put up for a yes or no when it is not, and correctable by hand either way.
  • Fingerprinting through AcoustID and MusicBrainz, and for the records no database has heard of, what the file's own path says it is.
  • A copy of every drive, with the audio hard-linked, and the player's own history read back into playlists.
  • Per-generation checks: a drive is checked against the oldest player it has to work on, and anything that player could not open is carried as a 320 kbps MP3 while the library keeps its lossless file.
02 — What is unproven, and what is simply missing

The parts to know about before a gig

Written is not the same as demonstrated. These are the gaps, stated the way they are stated in the project's own documentation rather than softened for a front page.

The gapWhere that leaves you
Nothing older than a CDJ-3000 has been seen to open a drive this writes Three players have: a CDJ-3000 on firmware 3.22, from the legacy export.pdb, and a CDJ-3000X and a CDJ-1500X, from the newer database. Getting there took eight rounds against the hardware, each a field a real export carries that this did not, after a CDJ-3000 on firmware 2.05 refused an earlier drive outright though the file parsed under two independent parsers. The last fixes were made together, so which of them a player needs is not known. Everything a nexus deck reads is written, and the database it reads is the one the CDJ-3000 now takes; whether one loads it has not been shown. Try a stick on the hardware before the night.
The waveform colours are a judgement call What rekordbox puts in those three bits for given audio is not published, and this has never been compared against a real export column by column.
Key detection is right about 37% of the time And confuses a key with its relative major or minor about another 18%. It is shown with its confidence for that reason. Treat it as a hint.
Cueing from the words costs minutes a track, twice over A separation and then a recogniser pass, and the recogniser is installed separately. What it hears is whatever Whisper hears: a heavily processed vocal or a chopped-up sample comes back as noise. Since 0.0.7 a lyrics server is asked first, which skips both steps when it answers, and what a track's words came from is recorded and shown.
One deck, and no mixing No pitch, no sync. It is for auditioning what you are preparing, not for performing.
Cues and grids are not written back into your files They live in the collection and on the drive. Names can be written back, on request.
Ogg, Opus and WMA are recognised but not converted Nothing in this build decodes them. Nothing resamples either: a file above 96 kHz is reported and left alone.

The project's own full list is longer and more technical, and it is the authority. Nothing on this page is meant to be gentler than it.

03 — Releases

Eight weeks, ten tags

0.1.129 October 2026

The octave from the genre, and cues that mean the same on every record

The genre, heard by a model you install or read off the file's own tag, says which octave the tempo is in, from a table of 76 club genres and the tempos they are played at. The beats go on the kick, a record at one tempo gets one straight grid through its breakdowns, and the one is read from the backbeat and the harmony where the kick cannot tell. Against madmom and Essentia on 75 CC records, Booth went from 52 of the 62 tempos they agree on, or a listener counted, to 58.

A to D mean the same on every record: the hook, the main break, the hook in a drop, the main drop. The words are read off the record cut to where its voice sings, a second separator, RoFormer, cleans that voice, and the lyric goes beside the track as an .lrc. Drive writes are landed whole, read back past the cache and stopped at the first fault, and booth-cli audit says what each player will see on any stick. There was no 0.1.0.

0.0.915 October 2026

A drive that plays on a CDJ-3000

Eight rounds against two players, each one a field a real export carries that this did not, until a CDJ-3000 on firmware 3.22 browsed a drive and played from it — from its slot and over Pro DJ Link — and a CDJ-3000X drew colour waveforms, hot cues and phrases from the same stick. The CDJ-3000 reads only the legacy export.pdb, and what changed was that file, brought to what every real export carries: a history row, page headers that describe their pages, a spare page for every table, and the browse menu's categories. Then the analysis files, down to the three-band waveform's scale, which had the player hang loading every track.

A Loudness setting, off by default: leave files alone, write ReplayGain tags, or re-encode to -14 LUFS beside the original. Only the last is one a CDJ hears.

0.0.87 October 2026

The beat grid corrected by hand, and the words where they can be read

Halve and double, nudge and shove, slide a beat onto the playhead, say which beat is the one, type the tempo or step it a tenth at a time, or mark beats and have a grid fitted to them. re-measure listens again for the tempo and the beats and nothing else.

The whole lyric in the inspector, each line at the moment it lands, and a hot cue read by the line it lands on. A CDJ-1500X on firmware 1.10 played a drive, which made two current players confirmed rather than one. And this website.

0.0.74 October 2026

The words looked up, not just listened for

A lyrics server asked before anything expensive — taken outright where the track was named by its own sound or where the lyric agrees with what was heard, put up for a yes or no where it is plausible and not certain, and editable by hand. A server saying a record is an instrumental saves the separation and the pass that would have ended in “nothing was sung”.

The recogniser's own doubt is recorded and shown when it is poor, its language is taken from what the collection decided on everything read so far rather than guessed from a breath at the top of a stem, and a stem is no longer rendered just to find out whether anybody sings on the track. No database page is ever written with a row cut in half.

0.0.62 October 2026

Grids that hold, and the line the room sings

A record at one tempo is gridded at one tempo however untidily its beats were tracked, so eight beats from anywhere in it are eight beats from anywhere else — which is what stops two loops from one track drifting apart on two players. Scatter and drift are told apart by their shape rather than their size.

What a track keeps saying is kept and shown: the lines it comes back to, how often each is sung, and where each one lands. Markers carry the whole sung line every time it lands, tested against a CDJ-3000X.

0.0.51 October 2026

Beat grids that hold still, and per-generation drives

A metronomic track stopped alternating between two wrong tempos every beat. Lyric cues moved onto the stem rather than onto Whisper's clock, which anchors its first segment at zero however long the intro runs. A drive is checked against the oldest player it has to work on, and music on a strange stick is copied into the collection by default.

0.0.429 September 2026

Every marker named, and drives that behave

Memory cues named and each return of a line marked. Eleven colour schemes. A second drive can be added and told apart from the first. A drive stopped re-copying itself every five minutes, and a grid that bends is kept with its own downbeat. Tracks that will be slow to reach are called out at import.

0.0310 September 2026

The waveform made legible

Drawn against the track rather than against full scale, as an envelope rather than a comb, with the loudest band first so the kick is visible at all. The phrase strip became correctable by hand, any section of the window can be sent to a window of its own, and switching between a track and its stems keeps your place.

0.0.25 September 2026

One name each, and all three of them Booth's

The rename: the engine, the command and the app settled on the names they have now. What a file's own path says it is became a source of names for the records no database has heard of, and tagging asks first when a file is named nothing like its tags.

0.014 September 2026

The first one

A copy of every drive written or plugged in, the player's history read back into playlists, the macOS app, the licence, and CI on every push. The point at which it was a program rather than a prototype.

04 — What is next

In rough order of how much it matters

  • A drive a nexus-generation player will open. Still the one that matters most and the one that needs hardware rather than code. The CDJ-3000 now plays from the legacy database every older deck reads, which is the best evidence there is short of one of them; but nothing older than a CDJ-3000 has been tried. A CDJ-2000NXS2, a stick, and a morning.
  • Correcting a bad reading by hand. Landed for the words in 0.0.7 and the beat grid in 0.0.8; the phrase strip has been correctable since 0.03. What is left is the rest of the analysis.
  • Archiving the drive copies. The linked half of a copy cannot be zipped, but the copied half can — once there are enough of them to see what they cost.
  • A second deck. The specification asks for one eventually. The deck here is for auditioning, and that is deliberate until it is not.

Issues and pull requests are on GitHub. The most useful thing anybody can send is a report from a player this has not been tried on — which deck, which firmware, and what it did with the stick.
